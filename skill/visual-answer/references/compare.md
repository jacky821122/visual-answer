# compare — choosing between options

The reader must decide. Give a recommendation and make the deciding differences obvious.

## Principles
- **Recommendation first**: which option, for which situation, and the one reason that decides
  it. If it is genuinely a taste call, say that plainly.
- **Show only the differences that decide it.** A compact matrix of the 3–6 dimensions the reader
  cares about, colour-coded by good/ok/bad with words in the cells. Dimensions that don't
  separate the options are cut or folded.
- **Explain the trade-offs in prose**: why those dimensions matter for this reader.
- **When to choose differently**: the conditions under which another option wins.
- **Losing options get a line or two each** on why not; the depth goes to the recommendation.
- **What the reader must decide** before you proceed, each with your suggested answer.
- Facts that age (prices, versions, popularity) carry a date in the footer.

## Examples (not required)
- A recommendation card with the winning option's name as the focal point.
- One small diagram per option only when their structure is the difference.

## Common mistakes
- Pros/cons per option with no verdict.
- Twenty matrix rows; invented scores out of 10.
