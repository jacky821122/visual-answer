# explain — how something works

The reader wants to *get it*: the one idea that makes everything else click, then enough depth
to reason about it themselves. Reading column, narrative flow.

## Principles
- **Answer first**, then the mental model: the parts and how they connect. This is where a
  diagram most often pays off (architecture, request path, lifecycle) — one or two per page is
  typical; a sequence of steps is usually a numbered list, not a diagram.
- **Go one level deeper per section**, headings phrased as the questions the reader would ask.
- **Ground it in the reader's own system**: their real config lines, commands and names beat
  generic examples. Mark briefly what you inferred rather than read.
- **Make the key constraint vivid.** Most systems are shaped by one or two constraints; give
  them a callout and explain the consequence ("所以 X 只能放在 Y").
- **Edges and misconceptions** near the end: where the model breaks, what people get wrong.
- If your source material contradicts itself, say so once, where it matters, and suggest how
  to check — it is useful to the reader, not a failure.

## Examples (not required)
- A one-sentence answer in a large lead, then a diagram with a short legend.
- A "為什麼不能…" section that walks through the constraint as a short chain.
- A small "常見誤解" list.

## Common mistakes
- Background or history before the answer.
- A diagram per section; a glossary repeating what the text already explained.
- Monotone page: no colour tied to meaning, no callouts for the key constraint.
